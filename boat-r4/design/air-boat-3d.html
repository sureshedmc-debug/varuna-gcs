How it collects

  1. The two BLDC motors on the plywood posts push air backwards, so the boat moves forwards with nothing turning in the water. An air fin behind each propeller steers the boat. Each fin has its own servo under it, and both servos get the same signal.
  2. The mesh ramp is hinged on a rod across the bow. Lowered, its front edge sits 2–3 cm under the surface. The two V-wings on its mouth catch a wider strip of water and steer floating waste onto the ramp.
  3. The brushes at the wing tips spin inwards and pull bottles and wrappers into the V. Weeds slip through the mesh.
  4. The ramp servo lifts the ramp: water drains through the mesh and the catch slides back towards the hinge.
  5. Tip into bin: lift it all the way and the catch slides over the bow into the bin, the open well in the middle of the hull. Then lower the ramp and keep collecting.
  6. Back at the bank, lift the mesh basket out of the well and empty it.

Shake-out (lift and drop twice) before tipping: loose weed falls back through the mesh, the plastic stays.

Upgrades to your build

  • V-wings on the ramp mouth: two plastic or mesh plates, about 22 cm long, angled 35° outwards and hinged to the ramp's side plates so they tilt with it. They make the catch about 1.6× wider.
  • Ramp servo out of the bin: move it from the well floor onto the bow rim. A horn and a 2 mm steel rod go to an arm on the ramp's side plate. The bin then stays free and the servo stays dry. Use a 20 kg·cm metal-gear servo.
  • Mesh basket in the well: it lifts out to empty, and water drains off the catch.
  • Air fins behind both propellers, each on its own small servo on an L-bracket off the post.
  • Prop guards (a ring of wire or a cut bottle) on both motors.
  • Splash cover over the breadboard, ESP32 and relays: a clear box lid. Better still, move the wiring from the breadboard to a soldered board: breadboard wires shake loose.
  • Phone on a short mast at the front of the electronics deck, camera looking over the ramp. Fix it firmly: the compass and gyro data depend on it.
  • Battery low and in the middle, just behind the bin under the deck. The tall posts and motors make the boat top-heavy.

Wiring on the ESP32

  • Ramp servo → GPIO26: 2000 µs lowered, 1000 µs raised. It glides with no jerk (AB_GATE_SPEED). In manual, VrB moves it. Turn VrB fully to "raised" to tip the catch into the bin.
  • Left fin servo → GPIO25, right fin servo → GPIO27. Both get the same signal from CH4.
  • Left ESC → GPIO32, right ESC → GPIO33.
  • Relay 1 → GPIO18: LED strip and buzzer (plastic alert). Relay 2 → GPIO19: brushes.
  • FS-iA6B PPM → GPIO23. The phone plugs into the ESP32's USB (Boat Link 2.3, which also records the logs).
